## 1. Thermal requirements for exterior doors (STR 2.01.02:2016) and achievable U values

### Takeaway
STR 2.01.02:2016 (order D1-754, amended incl. D1-23 of 2019-01-11; consolidated version in force from 2026-01-01) sets per-class reference U values. Several sources indicate doors/gates ≈ **1.4 (A), 1.3 (A+), 1.2 (A++) W/(m²K)** (the A++ row in the amended table reads "1,2 | 1,4·k | 1,7"), but the column meanings could not be read from the official text. When a private house only has its doors replaced (simple repair, "paprastasis remontas"), a 2020 interpretation says the new elements must meet the requirements set for elements in **class C** buildings. They do not have to meet A++. A well-made wooden door with triple glazing (Ug 0.5–0.6) can reach Ud ≈ 0.6–0.9. A door with double glazing (Ug ≈ 1.0–1.1) typically comes out around Ud ≈ 1.2–1.3, which is borderline for the A++ reference value.

### Inferences
- **Ud estimate for this door** (EN ISO 10077-1 area method, my calculation, not sourced):
  - Assumed sizes: frame ≈ 1.77 × 1.985 m = 3.51 m²; glass 2 × (0.64 × 1.62) ≈ 2.07 m² (≈ 59 %); glass edge length ≈ 9.0 m.
  - Pine 78 mm (Uf ≈ 1.2), Ug 0.5, ψ ≈ 0.045 → **Ud ≈ 0.9**.
  - Ug 1.0 double, Uf 1.2, ψ ≈ 0.05 → **Ud ≈ 1.2**.
  - Ug 1.1 double, Uf 1.31 (68 mm), ψ ≈ 0.05 → **Ud ≈ 1.3**.
  - These are optimistic: the threshold, the meeting stiles (stulp) of a double door and hardware usually raise Ud.
- **Double vs triple glazing:**
  - Triple glazing (Ug ≤ 0.6) in a 78 mm or thicker frame is the realistic way to stay at or below the A++ door reference (≈ 1.2) with margin.
  - Double glazing in a 68 mm frame is likely to land at about 1.3–1.4, which meets the C-class element requirement for a replacement but may fail A++ in a new build.
  - Large triple-glazed leaves are heavy (≈ 30 kg/m² of glass for 4/16/4/16/4), which is relevant for hinges and sagging.
- For an existing house where the doors are only replaced, the A++ door value is likely not mandatory, per the VTPSI interpretation. For a new A++ house, the door enters the building's energy calculation and its declared Ud must match the energy certificate project.

## 2. Safety glazing in doors

### Takeaway
Lithuanian regulation (previously STR 2.05.20:2006 "Langai ir išorinės įėjimo durys", now STR 2.04.01:2018 "Pastatų atitvaros. Sienos, stogai, langai ir išorinės įėjimo durys", in force from 2019-04-04 and amended up to 2024-11-27) defines "doors and areas around doors" as **critical positions**. Glass there must be "saugus stiklas" classified under **LST EN 12600**, with a minimum impact class depending on the glass size: class 2 if the smaller glass dimension is > 900 mm, class 3 if ≤ 900 mm (as read from the table snippet). The glass must also be permanently marked. For 61–67 cm wide glass (≤ 900 mm), the minimum is reportedly impact class 3. In practice, laminated (e.g. 33.1/44.2, class 1B1–2B2) or toughened (1C1) panes on both faces of the unit are best practice.

### Inferences
- Each pane here is ≈ 61–67 cm wide (≤ 900 mm) and reaches from about 15–30 cm above the floor to about 175–190 cm, so it sits in a critical position. A minimum of EN 12600 class 3 (per the snippet; many practitioners use class 2) applies to at least the side people can impact.
- Best practice for an entrance door: laminated glass on the outside (security, holds together), plus laminated or toughened on the inside. For example, a 44.2 / ESG triple or double unit gives both impact safety and some burglary resistance; EN 356 P2A/P4A is optional.
- Toughened-only (1C1) satisfies impact safety but gives little burglary resistance.

## 3. EN 14351-1 product standard and CE marking (what a custom joiner should declare)

### Takeaway
External pedestrian doorsets fall under harmonised **EN 14351-1:2006+A2:2016** (Construction Products Regulation 305/2011). A manufacturer placing them on the market must draw up a **Declaration of Performance (DoP)** and affix **CE marking**. Under AVCP **system 3** (doors not on escape routes), a notified lab does the type testing and the manufacturer runs Factory Production Control. Key characteristics are:
- watertightness (EN 1027 / EN 12208)
- air permeability (EN 1026 / EN 12207)
- wind resistance (EN 12211 / EN 12210)
- thermal transmittance Ud (EN ISO 10077-1/-2 calculation or EN ISO 12567-1 hot box)
- safety glazing / impact
- load-bearing capacity of safety devices
- dangerous substances
- for doors: height/width, operating forces and others where relevant

STR 2.04.01:2018 also requires doors to be designed for these properties, with minimum watertightness and wind classes per its tables.

### Cited Findings
**Standard and AVCP**
- EN 14351-1 sets material-independent performance characteristics for windows and external pedestrian doorsets, including unframed glass and escape-route doorsets. Fire and smoke properties are excluded and covered by EN 16034. It is harmonised under CPR (EU) 305/2011, which is the basis of CE marking. — [Intertek EN 14351-1](https://www.intertek.com/building/standards/en-14351-1/); [iTeh EN 14351-1:2006+A2:2016](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/5cf279d1-55d9-4267-b555-3b26db1679e1/en-14351-1-2006a2-2016)
- The 2006 edition was withdrawn and superseded by +A1/+A2 (2016). A revision prEN 14351-1 exists, but its status was not confirmed. — [Austrian Standards listing](https://austrian-standards.at/en/shop/onorm-en-14351-1-2006-08-01~p2409730); [prEN 14351-1 on iTeh](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/48c9330c-6ddb-46a3-9ef8-afbdf7ebf592/pren-14351-1)
- **AVCP system 1** applies to external pedestrian doors on escape routes (where the relevant characteristic is declared). **System 3** applies to external doors and windows not on escape routes, with type testing by a notified lab. — [UKAS schedule / UK guidance](https://www.ukas.com/download-schedule/27297/Testing/); [Profile22 CE marking FAQ](https://www.profile22.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/CE-Marking-FAQs.pdf)
- A sample DoP for an external door (system 3) lists watertightness (e.g. "non-shielded 6B"), wind-load resistance, dangerous substances, durability of self-closing etc. — [EuroWindoor Guidance Sheet CE.03 (DoP)](https://www.eurowindoor.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/CE03_1511_EN.pdf)
- The U value can be derived by three methods (tabulated, calculated, measured) and is declared as a value. — [SBS "common-sense approach to AVCP of windows and doors"](https://sbs-sme.eu/publication/common-sense-approach-assessment-and-verification-constancy-performance-avcp-windows-and/)
- An ift Rosenheim certification scheme exists for windows and external pedestrian doorsets. Small and medium manufacturers can use "shared/cascading" type testing results via system houses; details were not retrieved. — [ift certification scheme](https://www.ift-rosenheim.de/download-contractualbase?id=170); [European Aluminium CE guideline](https://european-aluminium.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/en-cpr-guidance-for-aluminium.pdf)

**STR 2.04.01:2018 requirements (LT)**
- Window and external door design must assess:
  - heat transmission
  - airborne sound insulation
  - wind-load resistance, set from the wind zone, terrain, height and position
  - watertightness (≥ the class in the table; classes per **LST EN 12208**: 0, 1A…9A, E; or 1B…7B for shielded; E > 600 Pa)
  - air permeability
  - mechanical strength
  - — [e-seimas STR 2.04.01:2018](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/lasupplement/TAP/8947cbf0aab011e8aa33fe8f0fea665f/309df8a0b1bb11e8aa33fe8f0fea665f/format/ISO_PDF/); [kbu.lt copy](https://kbu.lt/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/STR-2.04.012018.pdf)
- Windows and external doors must be **installed per the manufacturer's recommendations or its publicly available instructions** (STR 2.04.01:2018). — [kbu.lt STR 2.04.01:2018](https://kbu.lt/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/STR-2.04.012018.pdf)
- A 2024 project specification (not the legal minimum) uses wind class A1–A4, watertightness 4A–6A and air permeability class 3 for windows/doors. — [rkp.lt project 2024](https://www.rkp.lt/uploads/3-PG-24-201-TDP-SK.pdf)

### Inferences
- **What a custom joiner should declare** (DoP + CE):
  - Ud (calculated per EN ISO 10077-1/-2 for the actual size, or from cascaded tests)
  - air permeability class (target **class 3–4**, EN 12207)
  - watertightness class (inward-opening wooden double doors typically reach 3A–5A; outward-opening up to 7A–9A with good thresholds)
  - wind resistance class (e.g. C2/C3, EN 12210)
  - glass type with its EN 12600 class
  - dangerous substances "NPD" or none
  - for doors: impact resistance and load capacity of safety devices where declared
  - the reference to EN 14351-1:2006+A2:2016 and AVCP system 3 with the notified lab number
- Very small joiners often lack type tests. The buyer should at least ask for a calculated Ud for the specific size and a written spec of glass, seals and threshold. If the house aims for A++ certification, the energy assessor needs a declared Ud.

### Gaps
- The minimum class values in the STR 2.04.01:2018 tables (watertightness and wind by building height/zone) were not readable.
- No Lithuanian guidance was found on CE obligations for one-off custom doors. Whether a one-off made directly for a customer counts as "placing on the market", and the CPR Art. 5 derogation for individually manufactured non-series products, should be checked.

## 4. Thresholds, seals, water drainage, weatherboard, accessibility

### Takeaway
Best practice is a **thermally broken aluminium or composite threshold, ~20 mm high**, set on an insulating support (not directly on concrete), with damp-proofing under the frame/threshold. It should be combined with a weatherboard (drip) on the bottom rail. For a barrier-free design, a drop-down (Absenk-) seal plus a drainage channel in front can be used. Lithuanian accessibility practice and German DIN 18040 both cap thresholds at **20 mm**. Certified low-threshold systems achieve driving-rain class 7A–9A (EN 12208), mainly for outward-opening doors.

### Cited Findings

- Do not fix the exterior door threshold directly onto a concrete base, because it creates a cold bridge. Embed special PVC (support) profiles. - Barrier-free concept:

### Inferences
- **Recommended for this door:**
  - a ~20 mm thermally broken aluminium threshold with integrated drainage, sized to the frame depth (68/78/92 mm)
  - an insulating load-bearing support profile under it (e.g. PU/Purenit/XPS-type plinth profile)
  - a bitumen/EPDM membrane turned up behind the threshold
  - an aluminium or hardwood **weatherboard** (vandens nuvedimo profilis) with drip on the outside of each bottom rail
  - a drop-down seal (automatinis nuleidžiamas sandariklis) on each leaf
  - a vertical meeting-stile seal plus top and bottom flush bolts on the passive leaf
  - a canopy/porch roof, especially if the door opens inward
- STR 2.03.01:2019 accessibility rules in principle target public buildings and adapted dwellings. A private single-family house is likely not obliged, but 20 mm is the common best-practice ceiling anyway.

## 5. Installation ("šiltas montavimas" / RAL) and opening vs frame dimensions

### Takeaway
RAL-type installation uses three planes:
- inside: airtight / vapour-tight (vapour barrier tape)
- middle: thermal insulation (low-expansion PU foam)
- outside: driving-rain-tight but vapour-open (diffusion-open tape or compriband)

The frame is fixed with anchors or screws, and wedges are only temporary. STR 2.04.01:2018 legally requires installation per the manufacturer's instructions. The usual rule of thumb is a **~10 mm gap all round (up to ~15 mm)**, so the element is about 20 mm smaller than the opening in each direction. With a threshold bearing on the floor support, the bottom gap is about 0. For a 180 × 200 cm opening this gives a frame of roughly **177–178 × 198.5–199 cm**, so the proposed 177 × 198.5 is consistent.

### Cited Findings
**RAL three-plane principle**
- RAL principle: "innen dampfdicht, mittig luftdicht/gedämmt, außen schlagregendicht und diffusionsoffen". The components are inner sealing tape (vapour-tight), PU foam (insulation), and outer compriband (rain-tight, diffusion-open). Mounting wedges are temporary and removed after the foam cures. Large doors (> ~110 cm wide or > 226 cm high) need a structural check before mounting (forum claim, unverified). — [bau.de forum](https://bau.de/forum/fenster-tueren/12355.php); [bau.de Haustür RAL](https://bau.de/forum/fenster-tueren/10923.php)
- RAL guideline referenced as "Leitfaden zur Planung und Ausführung der Montage von Fenstern und Haustüren" (RAL-Gütegemeinschaft). An ift edition 3/2014 is cited; one vendor cites RAL-GZ 695 (2021). — [Würth Fenstermontage PDF](https://media.wuerth.com/stmedia/wuerth/documents/documents/LANG_de/28231924.pdf); [ift Rosenheim Fachartikel Fenstermontage](https://www.ift-rosenheim.de/documents/10180/1689819/FA_DZ_1908_Fenstermontage_Teil_1-3/3d3e5edb-1be8-d0a5-b128-87e9bb0780bb); [neu-west.com](https://neu-west.com/bauwissen/fenstereinbau-nach-ral/)

**Gap sizes and dimension deduction**
- Würth's sealing tapes cover joint widths from about 2–5 mm up to 10–25 mm, depending on type. There is no single fixed RAL gap. — [Würth PDF](https://media.wuerth.com/stmedia/wuerth/documents/documents/LANG_de/28231924.pdf)
- Rule of thumb: the element should be about **10 mm smaller than the masonry opening on every side**. Example: opening 1000 × 1500 → element 980 × 1480 mm. Order by element size, not opening size. — [Ventano Montageanleitung](https://www.ventano-fenster.de/anleitung-fenstermontage-holzfenster-ral-montage)
- A 6–15 mm compriband works only if the frame-to-reveal gap is **≤ 15 mm**. — [fertigfenster.de RAL-Montage](https://www.fertigfenster.de/ral-montage/)
- An LT installation guide recommends filling the gap between wall and frame with **low-expansion PU foam** as specified by the product maker. — [medasa.lt montavimo instrukcija](https://www.medasa.lt/zinotina/montavimo-instrukcija/); [Morita Stratus installation section PDF](https://morita.lt/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Stratus_montavimo-instrukcija-2021m-2.pdf)
- An LT source cites 2 mm (top/sides) and 4 mm (bottom) gaps. These are **leaf-to-frame clearances** for interior doors, not installation gaps, and should not be confused. — [allconstructions.lt "Durų pakeitimas"](https://lt.allconstructions.com/straipsniai/41086-duru-pakeitimas)

**Regulatory**
- STR 2.04.01:2018: windows and external doors are installed per manufacturer recommendations or publicly available instructions. — [kbu.lt STR 2.04.01:2018](https://kbu.lt/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/STR-2.04.012018.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Opening 180 × 200 cm → frame:**
  - Width: 1800 − 2 × 10…15 = **1770–1780 mm**.
  - Height (threshold bearing on the floor or support profile, gap only at top): 2000 − 10…15 = **1985–1990 mm**.
  - The proposed **177 × 198.5 cm** fits the 15 mm side/top gaps used with 6–15 mm compriband or foam plus tapes.
- If the finished floor or a support/insulating plinth profile under the threshold is not yet in place, the frame height must account for it. For example, a 30–50 mm support profile reduces the frame height accordingly, or the opening must start below finished floor level.
- **Leaves:**
  - Two leaves of 83 cm = 1660 mm; frame width 1770 leaves ≈ 55 mm per jamb, including the leaf rebate overlap.
  - This is plausible for a 68–78 mm frame with rebated leaves, but the joiner must confirm it against the profile drawing.
  - The clear opening with both leaves open will be about 1600–1650 mm; one active leaf gives only about **700–740 mm** clear width (inference).
- Fixings: for a ~3.5 m² wooden double door weighing about 120–180 kg with triple glazing, use frame anchors/screws near each hinge and lock keep (typically ≤ 700 mm spacing, about 150 mm from corners). Use load-bearing packers under the hinge jamb and threshold. These are general RAL practice values, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- The RAL guideline text itself (gap tables, fixing distances) was not accessible.
- No Lithuanian manufacturer page giving the opening→frame deduction for doors specifically was retrieved; the Medasa/Morita PDFs were not fetchable.

## 6. Inward vs outward opening for double entrance doors in Lithuania

### Takeaway
No authoritative Lithuanian source compares the two directions. Manufacturer material says outward-opening doors keep out driving rain better, are harder to force (the leaf bears against the frame) and are pressed shut by the wind, and they save indoor space. They need a canopy or porch so snow does not block the leaves, and hinges must be security-protected. Inward-opening doors are traditional, are never blocked by snow and keep hinges inside, but are harder to make rain-tight at a low threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Outward opening is described as most popular. It saves inner space, keeps water out in heavy rain, is harder to break in, and the wind presses it shut rather than tearing it open (manufacturer claims). — [doleta.lt "Lauko durys atidaromos į lauką"](https://www.doleta.lt/lt/produktai/lauko-durys/lauko-durys-atidaromos-i-lauka/)
- Security: inner (concealed) hinges make doors harder to remove; exterior doors use multipoint locks and extra bolts. — [ermitazas.lt](https://www.ermitazas.lt/blogas/lauko-durys-kaip-pasirinkti-tinkamas); [manonamai.lt](https://manonamai.lt/isores-apdaila/g-47160-sarvuotos-durys-privalumai-ir-trukumai)
- Barrier-free rain-tightness results (7A) are cited as "best value for barrier-free **outward-opening** doors". Low thresholds achieve better rain-tightness when opening outward. — [bba-online](https://www.bba-online.de/tueren/aussentueren/tuerschwelle-barrierefreie-haustuer-mit-nullschwelle/)
- Comparison of standard vs reverse (outward) opening exists in LT door-maker blogs. — [deltadurys.lt](https://deltadurys.lt/naujienos?journal_blog_post_id=18); [solisdurys.lt](https://solisdurys.lt/straipsniai/reversiniu-duru-privalumai-ir-trukumai)

### Inferences
- **Inward opening (common for wooden doors):**
  - needs a good weatherboard, a drop-down seal and a protected location (canopy)
  - space inside for two 83 cm leaves to swing
  - security hinges are not an issue
- **Outward opening:**
  - better driving-rain tightness and burglary resistance
  - leaves swing into snow and wind, so a canopy and a 1.0–1.5 m clear landing are needed
  - hinge pins need anti-lift security
  - leaves exposed to weather when open
- For double doors in Lithuania, outward opening also matches evacuation logic. Inward opening is acceptable for a private house (see §7).

### Gaps
- No independent or technical Lithuanian source compares opening directions or gives snow-related guidance.

## 7. Clear passage width and fire escape considerations (private house)

### Takeaway
No specific Lithuanian rule was found for the entrance door width or opening direction of a single-family house. Escape-route rules found (doors opening in the escape direction, minimum exit width 0.8 m for ≤ 15 people, width measured as clear passage inside the frame) apply to public and industrial buildings. Accessibility practice uses ≥ 850 mm clear width for adapted doors. One 83 cm leaf gives only about 70–74 cm clear width, so the passive leaf will need to be opened for furniture or wheelchairs.

### Cited Findings
- Fire-safety rules contain separate sections for residential buildings. The 0.8 m minimum escape exit width (for ≤ 15 people) and "doors open in the direction of evacuation" appear in rules for public, commercial and industrial buildings. — [PAGD consultations on public building fire rules](https://pagd.lrv.lt/media/viesa/saugykla/2023/11/zQgANqsvBpc.pdf); [Kaunas district project proposal](https://www.krs.lt/media/11057/projektiniai-pasi%C5%ABlymai.pdf); [Pasvalys](https://www.pasvalys.lt/naujienos/1/pagrindiniai-gaisrines-saugos-reikalavimai-prekybos-paskirties-pastatams-ir-patalpoms:7823)
- The evacuation door width is assessed by the internal frame dimension (clear passage). — [Klaipėda project proposal](https://www.klaipeda.lt/data/public/uploads/2019/07/projektiniai-pasiulymai.pdf)
- Accessibility practice: clear width of adapted doors ≥ 850 mm between the open leaf and the frame. — [Biržai project proposal](https://www.birzai.lt/data/public/uploads/2021/03/projektiniai-pasiulymai-1.pdf)
- EN 14351-1: escape-route doorsets are AVCP system 1, while ordinary external doors are system 3. — [UKAS](https://www.ukas.com/download-schedule/27297/Testing/)

### Inferences
- For a private single-family house, the double door is very unlikely to be an escape-route door in the regulatory sense, so system 3 / ordinary requirements apply. Opening direction is free.
- Practical recommendations:
  - make the active leaf the wider or main one
  - use passive-leaf flush bolts that are easy to release, so the full ~160 cm opening can be used
  - if accessibility matters, consider asymmetric leaves (e.g. 95 + 71 cm) to get ≥ 850 mm clear width on the active leaf

### Gaps
- The Lithuanian fire safety rules for residential buildings (Gaisrinės saugos pagrindiniai reikalavimai / STR 2.02.01 "Gyvenamieji pastatai") were not read. No explicit minimum entrance-door width for a single-family house was found.
