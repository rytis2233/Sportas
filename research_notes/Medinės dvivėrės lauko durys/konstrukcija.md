# Construction of a wooden exterior double door with large glass panels (opening 180 × 200 cm, Lithuania)

## 1. Typical frame (stakta / Blendrahmen) and leaf profile dimensions; leaf thickness standards (68 / 78 / 88 / 92 mm)

### Takeaway
Wooden window and door systems in Europe are named by profile depth: IV56, IV68, IV78, IV88 and IV92, where the number is the thickness (depth) in mm. Exterior wooden doors are typically 68, 78 or 92 mm thick. The frame face width I found for wooden door frames is about 55–90 mm, for example 55 × 92 mm or 85 × 90 mm. For a Lithuanian climate with triple glazing, a 78 mm leaf in a frame of about 78–92 mm depth is the mainstream choice. A 68 mm leaf is the minimum.

### Cited Findings
- **IV78 definition:** FAKRO describes IV 78 as a layer-glued (laminated) profile 78 mm thick, made of pine, Meranti or oak, with triple gaskets. — [FAKRO technical information](https://www.fakro.com/technical-information)
- **Laminated hardwood door maker, three leaf types (68 / 78 / 92 mm):**
  - The 78 mm model uses a "Natural Glulam Hardwood Frame" with a layer-glued inner frame for stability, and has U_D = 1.3 W/m²K.
  - The 68 mm system has a laminated hardwood frame profile of 85 × 90 mm, double-rebated.
  - Source: [block95.eu – Specifications modern hardwood front doors](https://block95.eu/specifications-modern-hardwood-front-doors/) (snippet only)
- **Another laminated timber entrance door:** frame cross-section 55 × 92 mm, leaf thickness 68 mm, triple glazing, U ≈ 1.0 W/m²K. — [Ecomerchant – Timber entrance doors](https://www.ecomerchant.co.uk/entrance-doors.html) (snippet only)
- **Bekru IV 78 wooden door:** laminated timber sections ("lamellierte Holzkanteln") for resistance to twisting, U_D = 1.0 W/m²K, security up to RC2. — [Bekru-Werke – Holztüren Bautiefe 78 mm](https://www.bekru-werke.de/holztueren-bautiefe-78-mm/)

### Inferences
- An IV78 leaf can take a triple unit of about 40–44 mm, given the 28–42 mm glass range for the IV78 window system and the 41 mm maximum glass for an 80 mm-wide door profile. With 44.2 laminated glass, a triple unit such as 44.2/12/4/12/4 or 44.2/14/4/14/4 is roughly 41–45 mm thick, which is at the limit for 78 mm and comfortable for 88/92 mm.
- For a frame in an unframed 180 × 200 cm opening, a wooden frame about 68–90 mm in face width × 78–92 mm in depth is realistic. A narrower frame face (about 55–60 mm) exists in some systems (55 × 92 mm).

## 2. Minimum widths of stiles, top rail, bottom rail and meeting stiles; is 80 mm feasible?

### Takeaway
I found no source documenting 80 mm stiles on a wooden exterior entrance door with a multipoint lock. The published reference minimum for a glass cut-out frieze is 120 mm (Lindner), and DIN-style interior cut-outs use 160 mm sides/top with a 400 mm bottom rail. The binding constraint is the lock side. A standard 35 mm-backset multipoint lock has a case depth of about backset + 16–20 mm, so about 51–55 mm, plus the glass rebate of about 18–22 mm. That needs roughly 75–80 mm of stile before any wood remains behind the lock case, so an 80 mm lock stile is essentially not workable with a standard lock.

An 80 mm stile becomes conceivable only with these changes:
- a narrow-backset tube-frame multipoint lock (25–30 mm backset);
- laminated timber with a steel or aluminium core;
- an 80 mm hinge stile and top rail, while keeping the lock or meeting stiles and the bottom rail wider.

### Cited Findings

- **Standard DIN-type glass cut-outs (interior doors):** three-sided frame of 160 mm with a bottom of about 400 mm. — [glastuershop24 – Lichtausschnitte](https://www.glastuershop24.de/innentueren-lichtausschnitte.html)

- **Solid-wood door example (40 mm interior leaf):** friezes 15 cm at the top and sides, 23 cm at the bottom rail. — [BM online](https://www.bm-online.de/allgemein/gestaltung-und-konstruktion-von-haustueren/) (snippet)

### Inferences (own reasoning from the cited dimensions)
- **Lock stile arithmetic with a standard lock** (35 mm backset, 16–20 mm faceplate):
  - The case reaches about 35 + 16…20 = 51–55 mm into the stile.
  - The glass rebate and bead take about 18–22 mm from the inside edge.
  - 51–55 + 18–22 = 69–77 mm, which leaves only 3–11 mm of timber in an 80 mm stile.
  - The lever/handle rosette and cylinder escutcheon also need flat face area around the 35 mm axis.
  - With a 92 mm-backset system, which some people confuse with the 92 mm centre distance, it is impossible.
  - Conclusion: an 80 mm lock-side stile is not feasible with standard 35 mm locks.
- **With a 25–30 mm backset tube-frame multipoint lock:** the case depth is about 41–50 mm. 80 − 20 (rebate) = 60 mm of solid timber is just enough. Even so:
  - the stile is heavily weakened at the lock height;
  - every locking point (hooks or bolts every ~500–600 mm) requires routing along the whole edge;
  - security/RC2 performance would be reduced.
- **Practical recommendation for a "narrow look":**
  - hinge stiles and top rail about 80–90 mm;
  - meeting stiles: active leaf about 100–110 mm, passive leaf about 90–100 mm, plus a rebated or overlapping meeting joint;
  - bottom rail about 150–200 mm, to allow the threshold seal and drop-down seal, and for splash and water exposure.
  - Alternatively, the meeting stiles of both leaves together read as one wider central "mullion" of about 180–200 mm. Many joiners accept this because the outside stiles then look slim.
- **To approach 80 mm everywhere:**
  - use 3-layer laminated hardwood (oak or meranti) with an embedded steel flat bar or tube, or an aluminium core;
  - use 88–92 mm leaf depth, which adds stiffness;
  - use a narrow-backset (25–30 mm) multipoint lock;
  - use bonded (structurally glued) glazing so the glass contributes to stiffness;
  - consider dispensing with a multipoint lock on the active leaf. Security and air-tightness then suffer, so this is not recommended for Lithuanian winters.
  - Expect a bespoke price and the need for a joiner experienced with this.

### Gaps
- I found no lock maker's (GU, KFV, Winkhaus, Maco) published "minimum stile width for wooden doors" figure.
- I found no TRADA, BWF or Wood Window Alliance guide numbers for minimum stile widths of glazed external doors.
- I could not verify typical professional exterior wood-door stile widths (commonly said to be about 110–130 mm, bottom rail 180–250 mm) from a primary source. The 120 mm (Lindner) and 160/400 mm (DIN interior cut-out) figures are the closest documented numbers.

## 3. Weight of insulated glass units and estimated leaf weight

### Takeaway
Float glass weighs 2.5 kg/m² per mm of thickness. Estimated unit weights and the resulting load per glass pane (about 1.0–1.1 m²):

| Unit | Weight per m² | Per pane (≈1.0–1.1 m²) |
|---|---|---|
| Triple 4/12/4/12/4 | ≈ 31 kg/m² | ≈ 30–34 kg |
| Double 44.2/16/4 | ≈ 32 kg/m² | ≈ 31–35 kg |
| Triple with one 44.2 pane | ≈ 42 kg/m² | ≈ 41–46 kg |
| Triple with two 44.2 panes | ≈ 53 kg/m² | ≈ 52–59 kg |

An 83 × 191 cm leaf with a triple unit with one 44.2 pane plus hardware therefore weighs about 60–80 kg in oak and 55–70 kg in pine.

## 4. Sagging risk and how it is prevented

### Takeaway
Sagging (the lock-side corner dropping) is controlled mainly in four ways:
1. Correct diagonal glazing blocks (setting blocks), which make the glass act as a rigid diagonal brace.
2. Strong corner joints: mortise and tenon, double tenon, or dowels in laminated timber.
3. Laminated (3-layer, lamellated) stile and rail stock, which resists twisting.
4. Hinges rated well above the leaf weight, with 3D adjustment to correct settling.

Mitred corners should not be used for load-bearing door frames.

### Cited Findings
- **Block functions:** support blocks (Tragklötze) carry the glass weight into the frame. Distance blocks (Distanzklötze) keep the glass off the rebate; depending on opening position they take over load. — [Baunetz Wissen – Klotzung](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/glas/fachwissen/lagern-verbinden/klotzung-159212); [gebaeudehuelle.net – Die Klotzung](https://www.gebaeudehuelle.net/fenster-glas/fenster-glas-fachartikel/die-klotzung-unscheinbar-aber-entscheidend)
- **Diagonal blocking for door infills:** lower support blocks on the hinge side, upper blocks on the lock side. Support blocks sit 40–100 mm from the rebate corner. — [pro-K Technisches Merkblatt – Verklotzung von Haustürfüllungen](https://www.pro-kunststoff.de/assets/Merkbl%C3%A4tter%20und%20Co/HTF%20Empfehlung-f%C3%BCr-die-Verklotzung-von-Haust%C3%BCrf%C3%BCllungen.pdf)
- **Block placement and thickness:** blocks about 100 mm from the glass corner for normal sizes and about 250 mm for large glazing; block thickness about 5 mm is typical. — [Montageprofis24 – Verglasungsklötze richtig einsetzen](https://www.montageprofis24.com/pages/verglasungsklotze-richtig-einsetzen); [Glas Wulfmeier memento 3.3 – Glasfalz und Klotzung von Isolierglas](http://www.glaswulfmeier.de/pdf/memento/memento2006_3.3_glasfalz_u_klotzung_von_isolierglas.pdf)
- **Block material and standards:** support blocks must not deform under the unit's weight, otherwise the glass is point-loaded. DIN 18545-1 covers glass edge engagement. — [gebaeudehuelle.net](https://www.gebaeudehuelle.net/fenster-glas/fenster-glas-fachartikel/die-klotzung-unscheinbar-aber-entscheidend)
- **Joints:**
  - Mortise and tenon is the classic and strongest frame-door corner joint; dowel or Lamello joints are easier but somewhat weaker. Load-bearing door frame corners are not mitred. — [clever-bauen – Rahmentür selber bauen](https://clever-bauen.de/ratgeber/rahmentuer-selber-bauen/)
  - Tenon thickness is usually about 1/3 of the timber thickness. Only about 2/3 of the tenon length should be glued so that timber movement does not open the joints. — [schreiner-seiten – Schlitz und Zapfen](https://www.schreiner-seiten.de/verbindungen/rahmen-schlitz-zapfen.php); [clever-bauen](https://clever-bauen.de/ratgeber/rahmentuer-selber-bauen/)
- **Laminated profiles:** used specifically for "hohe Verwindungsfreiheit" (resistance to twisting). — [Bekru-Werke](https://www.bekru-werke.de/holztueren-bautiefe-78-mm/); [FAKRO – IV78 layer-glued](https://www.fakro.com/technical-information)

### Inferences
- With 80 mm stiles, the corner joint is short. On a 78 mm-thick leaf, a tenon of about 26 mm thickness and only about 50–60 mm effective width is left after the rebate. The joint therefore carries the racking moment of a ~75 kg leaf cantilevered over about 0.83 m. Mitigations:
  - double tenons or 2–3 hardwood dowels of Ø 12–14 mm per corner, glued with D4 PVAc or PUR;
  - correct diagonal blocking, so the glass takes the diagonal compression;
  - a 3D hinge with vertical adjustment, to re-level after settling;
  - optionally a steel core in the lock or meeting stile.

### Gaps
- I found no wood-specific (e.g. ift Rosenheim) guideline on maximum glass weight versus stile cross-section, and no quantitative sag criteria (e.g. allowable drop in mm).

## 5. Hinges: number and type for 60–100 kg wooden exterior leaves

### Takeaway
The standard choice for wooden entrance doors is 3D-adjustable surface-mounted roller hinges, either Simonswerk VARIANT VX or Dr. Hahn equivalents. Manufacturers rate the load for two hinges per leaf (Simonswerk: 160–200 kg per pair for the VX 7729/7939 series). In practice exterior doors use 3 hinges per leaf. For 70–100 kg glazed leaves that gives a large safety margin, and the third hinge also limits bowing of the leaf.

- For a ~75–95 kg leaf: 3 × Simonswerk VARIANT VX (wood, rebated or unrebated version to match the profile), or an equivalent Dr. Hahn or Anuba 3D roller hinge in the wood version, rated at least 120–160 kg per pair.
- **Hinge stile width:** the VX roller hinge mounts on the leaf face or rebate with screws into the leaf edge zone. An 80 mm hinge stile is generally workable here, unlike the lock side, but the joiner must check fixing distances against the Simonswerk installation template.
- **Position:** commonly the top hinge about 200–250 mm from the top and the bottom hinge about 250–300 mm from the bottom, with the third hinge either centred or about 300 mm below the top hinge. This is general practice and was not verified in this session.

## 6. Timber species, laminated vs solid, moisture content, finishing systems and maintenance

### Takeaway
Exterior wooden doors in Central/Northern Europe are made of 3-layer laminated (lamellated, often finger-jointed in the inner layer) stock. Common species are pine or spruce, larch, oak and meranti. Oak and meranti are the more weather-resistant options. Wood moisture is around 12–13 % ± 2 %. Industrial water-based coating systems (Remmers Induline, Teknos, Sikkens, Adler) claim 8–10+ years before re-coating, provided maintenance with care products is done every ~2 years. Semi-transparent stains on weather-exposed sides need re-coating more often (2–3 years) than opaque paint (4–5 years).

### Cited Findings
- **Species:** Eiche (oak), Lärche (larch) and Meranti are standard choices for front doors; meranti and oak are recommended as particularly resistant. Larch is a heavy softwood with strong grain. — [Türenheld – Holz-Haustüren](https://www.tuerenheld.de/haustueren/holz/); [Türenheld – Holzarten für Türen](https://www.tuerenheld.de/blog/Holzarten-fuer-Tueren-So-waehlst-du-richtig)
- **IV78 timber options:** pine, meranti or oak, layer-glued. — [FAKRO](https://www.fakro.com/technical-information)
- **Three-layer glued meranti door profile:** "Hochwertige dreischichtverklebte Profile". — [fensterblick – Haustür Meranti](https://www.fensterblick.de/haustuer-meranti.html)
- **Wood moisture:** one manufacturer controls wood moisture at 13 ± 2 %; drying must be gentle to avoid warping and cracks. — [fensterblick – Holzhaustüren](https://www.fensterblick.de/holzhaustueren.html) (search summary; exact page attribution uncertain)
- **Densities (relevant to weight and hardness):** oak mean ~710 kg/m³, larch ~600 kg/m³, meranti 540–760 kg/m³ at 12–15 % MC. — [LfL Bayern](https://lfl.bayern.de/mam/cms04/waldschutz/dateien/das_holz_der_laerche_w69.pdf); [Teknos – Dark red meranti](https://www.teknos.com/globalassets/teknos.de/holzinformationsblatter/dark-red-meranti_03_de.pdf)
- **Remmers Induline:**
  - Premium coatings: "garantiert mindestens 10 Jahre keine Sanierungsanstriche" (guaranteed at least 10 years without renovation coats), conditional on cleaning and applying the care balm about every 2 years.
  - The guarantee relies on certified window fabricators.
  - An older Induline UV-system claim was at least 8 years coating-free under normal weathering.
  - Sources: [Remmers – RSG Fenster System-Garantie](https://www.remmers.com/de/rsg-fenster); [Remmers AT – Beschichtungen für Fenster & Holzelemente](https://www.remmers.at/de_AT/holzfarben-lacke/beschichtungssysteme-fuer-fenster-tueren-aus-holz/c/c672); [GLASWELT – Remmers Induline UV-Langzeitschutz](https://www.glaswelt.de/glaswelt-spezial/remmers-induline-uv-langzeitschutz-fuer-holzfenster)
- **Remmers products:** Induline I-900 (opaque topcoat for windows and doors); Induline LW-725 (transparent stain topcoat). — [Remmers – Induline product page](https://www.remmers.com/de/holzfarben-lacke/beschichtungssysteme-fuer-fenster-tueren-aus-holz/lasuren/induline-t-785-premium/p/000000000000394105)
- **Maintenance intervals:** about 2 years for intact surfaces; small film damage must be repaired immediately. — [holzschutz-genial – Holzfenster und Holztüren mit Remmers lasieren](https://holzschutz-genial.de/blog/holzfenster-und-holztueren-mit-remmers-holzschutzmittel-lasieren)
- **Meranti windows:** re-coat translucent stains every ~2–3 years and opaque coatings every ~4–5 years; annual visual check of the weather side. — [fensterversand – Farben Holz](https://www.fensterversand.com/hebeschiebetueren/holz/farben.php) (window retailer; rough guidance)
- **Guarantee disputes:** a forum case shows hail damage treated as outside the guarantee. — [BAU.DE forum](https://bau.de/forum/fenster-tueren/14094?m=Holzfenster)

### Inferences
- **For narrow 80–90 mm stiles:** dense, stiff hardwood laminated in 3 layers (oak, or meranti/sipo) is strongly preferable to pine, giving higher stiffness and screw-holding for the lock and hinges.
- **Lithuanian climate:** a factory-applied, water-based, 3-coat system (impregnation + primer + topcoat, total dry film about 80–120 µm), opaque or medium-dark stain, plus a canopy over the door to reduce weathering. Typical film thickness was not sourced.
- **Oak with stains:** iron fittings can stain oak (tannins), so use stainless steel screws and hardware.

## 7. Leaf sizes for two leaves within the 180 × 200 cm opening: gaps, frame, rebate overlap, gap between leaves

### Takeaway
The fitting gap between the masonry opening and the frame is about 10–15 mm per side. One manufacturer's RC2 guide gives 15 mm as the maximum, filled with insulation per RAL installation practice. This gives:
- outer frame size of about 1770–1780 × 1985–1990 mm (with threshold on the finished floor);
- with a frame face of about 60–70 mm, a clear passage of about 1640–1660 mm;
- two leaves of about 830–840 mm each (including rebate overlaps) and about 1900–1920 mm high.

These match the 83 × 191 cm assumption in the question.

### Cited Findings
- **Installation gap:** an RC2 door installation guide allows a maximum installation joint of 15 mm between the building opening and the frame, insulated per RAL guidance. — [neue-haustuer.de – RC2 Montageanleitung](https://www.neue-haustuer.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/RC2-T%C3%BCren-Montageanleitung.pdf)
- **Sizing method:** frame outer size + 2 × 10–15 mm per side = opening size; joint widths of 10–20 mm are typical by sealant group. This was not verified against RAL-GZ 695 itself. — [haus-selber-bauen – RAL-Montage](https://www.haus-selber-bauen.com/ral-montage.html)
- **Joint fill:** PU foam or mineral wool, with the joint to the wall sealed. — [haustueren-doors.de – Leitfaden zur Montage von Haustüren](https://www.haustueren-doors.de/pdf/Montage_CE.pdf)
- **Double door layout:** an active leaf (Gehflügel) plus a passive leaf (Standflügel) secured by bolts. — [haustuerenland – Zweiflügelige Nebeneingangstüren](https://www.haustuerenland.de/nebeneingangstueren/doppelfluegelig/)

### Inferences (own sizing example; confirm with the chosen profile drawings)

**Width**

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Frame outer width | 1800 − 2 × 12 | ≈ 1776 mm (round to 1775) |
| Frame jamb face | e.g. 68 mm, incl. ≈ 20 mm leaf rebate | 2 × 68 = 136 |
| Clear between jambs | 1775 − 136 | ≈ 1639 mm |
| Leaf overlap into jamb rebate | ≈ 12–15 mm each side | — |
| Meeting stile overlap | ≈ 12–15 mm | — |
| Total leaf width (sum of both) | 1639 + 2 × 13 + 13 | ≈ 1678 mm |
| Each leaf | 1678 / 2 | ≈ 835–840 mm |

**Height** (no top gap issue if the frame head is fixed to the lintel; 10–15 mm fitting gap at the top; a low aluminium/wood threshold about 20 mm proud of the finished floor; 3–4 mm air gap above the threshold)

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Frame outer height | 2000 − 12 | ≈ 1988 mm |
| Head rebate overlap | 68 mm head face, ≈ 13 mm overlap | — |
| Leaf height | 1988 − 68 + 13 − threshold zone (≈ 20–35 mm) | ≈ 1900–1915 mm |

- **Clear passage:** with the passive leaf bolted, the clear passage through the active leaf alone is about 1639 / 2 minus the meeting-stile intrusion, so about 760–790 mm. That is acceptable but not wide; opening both leaves gives about 1600 mm.
- **Glass size per leaf:**
  - With 80 mm stiles and top rail and a 150 mm bottom rail: visible about 675 × 1675 mm, glass about 695 × 1695 mm (≈ 1.18 m²). In triple with one 44.2 pane this is ≈ 50 kg of glass.
  - With 110 / 110 / 200 mm: visible about 615 × 1600 mm, glass about 635 × 1620 mm (≈ 1.03 m²), ≈ 43 kg.
- **Threshold:** for Lithuania, use a thermally broken low threshold with an EPDM seal plus a drop-down seal or a stepped (rebated) bottom rail to keep out driving rain. This reinforces the need for a bottom rail of at least about 150 mm.

### Gaps
- No Lithuanian-specific installation standard was found. LST EN 14351-1 and the Lithuanian building regulations (STR) on entrance-door thermal requirements (U-value) were not researched in this session. Check them if a building permit or energy class (A+/A++) applies.
- I found no source for the standard gaps between leaf and frame (commonly 3–4 mm on wooden doors) or the meeting-stile overlap. The values above are typical joinery assumptions.
