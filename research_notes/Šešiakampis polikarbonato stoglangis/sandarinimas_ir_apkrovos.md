## 1. Apex / top cap: options, sealing, trimming and taping the sheet tips

### Takeaway
No manufacturer or forum source found describes a polycarbonate-pyramid apex cap step by step; the cap should be treated as a mechanical/UV/cosmetic cover over a separately sealed apex (taped tips plus sealant/gasket), not as the only waterproofing layer. Commercial pyramid lanterns put clip-on ridge/hip caps on last, after the glazing clamps are fixed.

### Cited Findings
- Brett Martin's pyramid roof lantern installation guide sequence (snippet): "Align and clip on hip outers… Align and clip on ridge outer and fix down glazing clamps" comes before "Clip on ridge caps", with hip caps listed afterwards, i.e. caps are the last, clip-on layer over clamped glazing — [Brett Martin TB431 roof lantern installation](https://www.brettmartin.com/docs/default-source/daylight-systems-documents/technical-documents/tb431-installation-brett-martin-roof-lantern.pdf) (snippet only)
- Roof-Maker publishes separate pyramid rooflight install guides (flat-pack and fully assembled); content not seen — [Roof-Maker pyramid install guide 2024](https://roof-maker.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/241112-Pyramid-spec-install-fully-assembled-WEB-IG024-REV002.pdf); [flat-pack 2020](https://roof-maker.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Pyramid-spec-Installation-Guide-flat-pack-WEB-jan-2020.pdf) (title only)
- A finial/roof-cap supplier warns that decorative caps are "used for ornamental purposes only and not for supporting a finial or weathervane or sealing a roof from weather", and that the roof should be "properly sealed with roofing material before installing the cap" — [Ferro Weathervanes: installing roof finials or caps](https://ferroweathervanes.com/installing-roof-finials-or-roof-caps/) (snippet)
- Aluminium base-and-cap system guides for multiwall: lay "an initial bead of sealant and cap. Then cap with same sealant after 72 hours"; do not put silicone under or over cap screws; use "a high quality polyethylene backer rod and a 100% silicone that is compatible with polycarbonate" — [Amerilux base & cap install guide](https://ameriluxinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/pdf-downloads/install-guides/amerilux-base-cap-install-guide.pdf); [Verolite/Domtek base & cap guide](https://www.domtek.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/verolite-base-cap-install-guide-domtek.pdf) (snippets; attribution between the two near-identical guides uncertain)
- Same guides: "seal the upper edge with aluminum tape when the top of the sheet can't be seen from below"; they specify Dow 999-A which "bonds to polycarbonate and metals, not wood" — [Amerilux](https://ameriluxinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/pdf-downloads/install-guides/amerilux-base-cap-install-guide.pdf) (snippet)
- General multiwall rule (Russian/Ukrainian supplier guides): "upper ends are closed with solid tape, lower ends with perforated tape, all joints treated with silicone sealant"; solid tape stops condensate and dust entering the cells — [polikarbonat.net.ua: sealing ends of cellular PC](https://polikarbonat.net.ua/ru/blog/germetizaciya-torcov-sotovogo-polika/); [Carboplast install instructions](https://carboplast.ru/poleznye-sovety/instrukciya-po-montazhu-sotovogo-polikarbonata/) (snippets; attribution uncertain)
- Polycarbonate supplier: silicone "should never enter the channels" — [PolycarbonateStore install guide](https://www.polycarbonatestore.com/pages/how-to-install-multi-wall-polycarbonate-panels) (snippet)
- Ready-made polycarbonate pyramid tops (VELUX) are fixed with a "tamper resistant base… over pre-drilled holes in dome or pyramid" with security screws into a timber builder's kerb, tightened to a specified torque to avoid over-tightening — [VELUX polycarbonate top installation](https://contenthub.velux.com/api/public/content/30ec5-32a9f-83426_downloadOriginal) (snippet)
- Clear Amber publishes a DIY blog "6 types of roof lantern you can make yourself" (content not seen) — [Clear Amber Shop blog](https://clearambershop.com/en-us/blogs/trade-and-diy-blog/6-types-of-roof-lantern-you-can-make-yourself-for-less) (title only)

### Inferences
- Practical apex build-up for six converging triangles (synthesised from the general rules above, not from a pyramid-specific source): (1) stop the sheet tips ~20–40 mm short of the true apex so they never touch each other or the hip rafter hub (thermal movement), leaving a small hexagonal hole over the rafter hub; (2) seal every cut flute edge at the tip with solid (non-vented) aluminium tape folded over the edge — at the apex all flutes are "top ends"; (3) bed a hexagonal/round sheet-metal or spun-aluminium cap on a compressible EPDM/neoprene foam ring or a continuous bead of polycarbonate-compatible neutral silicone, overlapping each sheet by ≥50–75 mm; (4) fix the cap with one central screw (or bolt with EPDM washer) into the hub block where the hip rafters meet, not through the sheets; (5) let the cap skirt "float" so sheets can expand underneath.
- Spun aluminium caps/finials are sold as decorative items; per Ferro's warning, waterproofing must come from the tape + gasket/sealant underneath, with the cap shedding water and shading the tape from UV.
- A clear polycarbonate mini-dome over the apex is possible but adds another joint; a metal cap is simpler and hides the taped tips (aluminium tape is not long-term UV-stable if exposed — not verified in sources).
- Because solid tape is used at the tips and the flutes run downslope, any moisture inside the cells drains to the vented eave — this is why flute direction must be eave-to-apex (see Q3).

## 2. Hip caps: bent aluminium flashing, adjustable-angle cappings, EPDM gaskets, fixing

### Takeaway
The standard approach is a two-part "rafter bar": a base gasket on the timber hip rafter, sheets laid with a gap over the rafter centre, and a screw-down aluminium cap with EPDM gaskets fixed through the gap into the rafter — so the clamp, not a screw through the sheet, holds the panel. For this pyramid the cap must be bent to a ~35° fold (legs at ~145°), which falls inside the range of commercial ridge/hip profiles.

### Cited Findings
- Sunwood screw-down hip bar: powder-coated aluminium top cap with detachable cover cap, integrated "high-side EPDM gasket" sealing on top of the glazing plus a base gasket on the timber rafter; the cap is "a broad angled profile meant to accommodate changes in pitch"; fixed at roughly 300 mm intervals — [TrulyPVC Sunwood screw-down hip bar](https://www.trulypvc.com/conservatory-parts/conservatory-roof-bars/screw-down-bars/sunwood-screw-down-glazing-hip-bar) (snippet)
- Capex 70 hip bar & gasket: listing gives pitch range 15–50° but also a conflicting "minimum 5 / maximum 20°" — [Builders Dept Capex 70 hip bar](https://buildersdept.com/product/capex-70-hip-bar-gasket-3m-brown/) (snippet; internal conflict in listing)
- Birchwood P501 rafter system: 50 mm rafter gasket screwed to the rafter, screw-down aluminium capping bar clamps the multiwall sheet; for hips the maker recommends a 60 mm hip rafter and its dedicated hip system rather than forcing the standard bar to an angle; over-tightening "undulates the cap, limits expansion and compresses the sheet" — [Birchwood P501 installation](https://www.birchwoodtrading.co.uk/SiteDownloads/Rafter%20Glazing%20Bar%20Systems/P501%20Rafter%20Glazing%20Bar%20System/P501%20System%20Installation.pdf) (snippet)
- Birchwood P600 snap-fit: recommends a minimum 5° slope for multiwall so rain drains to the gutter — [Birchwood P600 installation](https://www.birchwoodtrading.co.uk/SiteDownloads/Structural%20Glazing%20Bar%20Systems/P600%20Snap-fit%20Structural%20Glazing%20Bar%20System/P600%20System%20Installation.pdf) (snippet)
- Russian polycarbonate ridge (конёк) profiles are for joining panels "at angles from 90 to 150 degrees" and compensate thermal expansion; a separate source says panels must not be pushed tight to the profile spine — the profile's internal space between sheets is "not less than 40 mm" — [Kinplast ridge profile](https://kinplast.ru/komplektuyushhie-dlya-polikarbonata/konkovyj-profil-iz-polikarbonata/); [Polygal Vostok: ridge installation](https://polygalvostok.ru/blog/montazh-konka-s-konkovym-profilem/) (snippets; which source said which is uncertain)
- Amerilux/Verolite base-and-cap system covers ridge and hip flashing details for multiwall; its standard method screws through the sheet at ~12 in (≈300 mm) centres — [Amerilux](https://ameriluxinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/pdf-downloads/install-guides/amerilux-base-cap-install-guide.pdf) (snippet)
- Thermal movement allowance: about 1/8 in per 4 ft (≈3 mm per 1.2 m) — [Gallina installation guide (AC Plastics)](https://www.acplasticsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Gallina-Installation-Guide-Edited-combined.pdf) (snippet)
- Sealant compatibility: Soudal Silirub PC (neutral, low-modulus) "does not cause stress cracking" in polycarbonate; but both Soudal and Wacker warn that contact with plasticiser-releasing rubbers ("EPDM, neoprene and butyl") can cause discolouration and loss of adhesion of the silicone — [Soudal Silirub PC TDS](https://media.trulypvc.com/pdfs/Soudal%20Silirub%20PC%20-%20Technical%20Data%20Sheet.pdf); [Wacker Lucky-Silicone LC 798](https://www.wacker.com/h/en-us/c/lucky-silicone-lc-798/p/000002183) (snippets)

### Inferences
- Hip geometry for this build: hip length ≈ 1.21 m, hip pitch ≈ 33.6°, fold angle between faces ≈ 35.4° (cap legs at ≈144.6° to each other). A flat aluminium strip (e.g. 1–1.5 mm, ~100–120 mm wide) bent on a brake to ~35° is feasible; commercial "broad angled" hip caps and 90–150° ridge profiles also fit this angle.
- Recommended detail: hip rafter top bevelled to the two face planes (or a flat-topped rafter ≥60 mm wide per Birchwood's hip advice) → EPDM base gasket/tape on each bevel → sheets with taped side edges, leaving a ~10–20 mm gap over the rafter centre-line → bent aluminium cap with EPDM strip/gasket on its underside legs → stainless screws with EPDM washers at ~300 mm through the gap into the rafter (3–4 screws per 1.21 m hip). The sheets are held by clamping, never pierced.
- Torque lightly: P501's warning about over-tightening applies; screws should compress the gasket, not crush the 20 mm sheet.
- Keep silicone away from direct contact with EPDM where the silicone must adhere (Soudal/Wacker warning) — use EPDM as the compression seal and silicone only as a secondary bead on metal/PC.
- Because the hip edges of the triangles cut across the flutes obliquely, every hip edge exposes open cells — these must get solid aluminium tape before the cap goes on (they are effectively "top/side ends").

### Gaps
- No datasheet found for a purpose-made adjustable-angle polycarbonate hip cap for 20 mm sheet at ~35° fold; no pull-out/clamp-force guidance for screw spacing under Lithuanian wind uplift.

## 3. Eave detail: overhang, drip, drainage from flutes, sealing to the bevelled bearer

### Takeaway
Supplier guides converge on: flutes running downslope, solid tape at top, vented/breather (anti-dust) tape at the bottom covered by an aluminium U/drip profile with weep holes, and an overhang of no more than ~75 mm (3 in) past the support. A gutter is not essential on a small pyramid if the drip clears the curb, but the drip line must not dump water on the curb flashing.

### Cited Findings
- Maximum overhang "3 inches from the structure" (≈75 mm) — [Onduline/Tuftex: how to install multiwall polycarbonate](https://us.onduline.com/en/diy/planning/news/news/how-install-multiwall-polycarbonate-panels-roofing); "do not exceed 3 inches of overhang" — [AC Plastics installation guide](https://www.acplasticsinc.com/media/documents/Installation%20Guide.PDF) (snippets)
- AC Plastics recommends aluminium or vented tape on top and bottom of flutes — [AC Plastics installation guide](https://www.acplasticsinc.com/media/documents/Installation%20Guide.PDF); Gallina: aluminium tape along the top flutes and vented tape along the bottom — [Gallina guide](https://www.acplasticsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Gallina-Installation-Guide-Edited-combined.pdf) (snippets)
- AntiDUST tape is a non-woven seal for top and bottom edges keeping out mould, algae, insects and dust — [Plaskolite fabrication guide](https://plaskolite.com/docs/default-source/fab/fab008_pgl_us.pdf); breather tape lets condensation evaporate and goes on the lower end, nearest the gutter — [LivSupplies](https://livsupplies.co.uk/?p=15347) (snippets)
- Aluminium U-profile to seal sheet edges, with weep holes drilled for condensation drainage — [Verolite/Domtek guide](https://www.domtek.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/verolite-base-cap-install-guide-domtek.pdf) (snippet; attribution between Amerilux/Verolite uncertain)
- Clear Amber's AXIOME system uses a proprietary end-stop bar over the breather tape — [search summary referencing Clear Amber AXIOME guide](https://www.clearamber.com/brochures/1480678805AXIOMEINSTALLERSGUIDETOCONDENSATIONV1.01WEBV2.pdf) (snippet)
- "Drip trim quality is one of the most critical factors"; PVC trims "will loosen, warp or break" over time while aluminium trims offer far greater strength — [Clear Amber 6 steps for preventing condensation](https://www.clearamber.com/news/44/6-EASY-STEPS-FOR-PREVENTING-CONDENSATION.html) (snippet; attribution to Clear Amber likely but not certain)
- Correct set-up is aluminium tape at top, breather tape at bottom; reversed or blocked tape lets condensation form in the chambers — [PolycarbonateX condensation guide](https://polycarbonatex.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-stop-condensation-in-multiwall-polycarbonate-roofing-sheets-complete-guide) (snippet)
- Silicone should never enter the channels; UV-protected side faces out; leave protective film on until installation is finished — [PolycarbonateStore](https://www.polycarbonatestore.com/pages/how-to-install-multi-wall-polycarbonate-panels) (snippet)
- Access rooflight guide asks for a maximum 70 mm kerb width "so the drip detail is not compromised" — [Roof-Maker access opening guide](https://roof-maker.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Access-Opening-spec-Installation-Guide-v2.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Flute orientation: cut each triangle so flutes run perpendicular to the eave (fall line, eave→apex). Water and condensate in the cells then drain to the eave; the two hip edges cut cells obliquely and the tip cuts them too — all sealed with solid aluminium tape. Only the eave edge gets vented/anti-dust tape + aluminium U/end profile with 2–3 weep holes (Ø ~5–6 mm) per side.
- Overhang: 30–50 mm past the outer face of the curb is consistent with the ≤75 mm limit and keeps the drip outside the curb cladding/flashing; the eave edge of 20 mm sheet is stiff enough at that projection.
- Seal between bevelled bearer (curb top plate) and sheet: EPDM sponge/foam glazing tape (compressible, lets sheet slide) is preferable to a rigid silicone bond; if silicone is used, use a polycarbonate-rated neutral silicone (e.g. Silirub PC type) and keep it out of the flutes. Butyl tape adheres well but per Soudal/Wacker is a plasticiser-bearing rubber — keep it away from silicone joints.
- Gutter: on a 2.65 m² plan pyramid, the rainwater per face is small and pitch is steep (37.5°); a drip edge throwing water clear of the curb onto the main roof (which must be able to take it) is normally sufficient. A gutter in Lithuania would also fill with ice; not recommended unless the drip lands on something vulnerable (judgement, not sourced).
- Hold-down at the eave: since the sheet must not be pierced, use the hip caps plus a continuous aluminium eave angle/clamp screwed into the curb through slotted holes, or screws through oversize (sheet-expansion) holes with EPDM washers if clamping alone is insufficient.

### Gaps
- No pyramid-specific eave detail (triangular sheet corner where two hips meet the eave) was found; corner closure and mitre of the U-profile is left to judgement.

## 4. Curb / upstand flashing to the main roof, insulation of the curb

### Takeaway
Rooflight manufacturers and forums consistently use a minimum 150 mm upstand above the finished roof surface, with the main roof covering (or a separate flashing) taken up the side and over the top of an insulated upstand; 3–5° fall is quoted for flat rooflights but irrelevant to a 37.5° pyramid. Curbs are significant cold bridges, so insulating them matters for condensation.

### Cited Findings
- Brett Martin pyramid lantern: "min. 150mm height upstand" and "roof covering must cover side and top of insulated upstand" — [Brett Martin TB431](https://www.brettmartin.com/docs/default-source/daylight-systems-documents/technical-documents/tb431-installation-brett-martin-roof-lantern.pdf) (snippet)
- Outlook polycarbonate pyramid rooflights come with a 150 mm high uPVC upstand — [SpecifiedBy: Outlook pyramid rooflights](https://www.specifiedby.com/rooflights-and-glazing/outlook-polycarbonate-flat-roof-pyramid-rooflights) (snippet)
- Ultraframe kerb: 70 mm width, 150 mm height at lowest point; PVCu kerb U-value 0.89 W/m²K, accommodates up to 200 mm roof insulation — [TrulyPVC Ultraframe Ultrasky kerb](https://www.trulypvc.com/roofing/sky-lights/ultraframe-ultrasky-kerb) (snippet; U-value attribution uncertain)
- A good timber upstand should extend 150 mm above roof level — [Skylights.co.uk forum: installing a skylight on a flat roof](https://skylights.co.uk/forum/question/how-to-install-a-skylight-on-a-flat-roof/) (snippet)
- Pre-made OSB kerb: 15 mm OSB with 40 mm insulation core — [Skylights.co.uk insulated upstand](https://skylights.co.uk/?p=34099) (snippet)
- Camden Council detail drawing: main roof membrane lapped up the kerb, at least 150 mm; 3–5° minimum fall for flat glazing — [Camden planning document](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/9423660/file/document) (snippet)
- BuildHub forum EPDM upstand: framed in 95 × 45 mm, 70 mm PIR between studs, lapped with PIR under the EPDM so 150 mm upstand remains exposed; skylights noted as significant cold bridges — [BuildHub: skylight upstands](https://forum.buildhub.org.uk/topic/26805-skylight-upstands/); [BuildHub: how to build insulated upstand](https://forum.buildhub.org.uk/topic/15005-how-to-build-insulated-upstand) (snippets; which thread uncertain)
- VELUX polycarbonate tops: supplied fixings are designed for a timber builder's kerb — [VELUX guide](https://contenthub.velux.com/api/public/content/30ec5-32a9f-83426_downloadOriginal) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For the existing hexagonal timber curb: check that the top plate is ≥150 mm above the finished main-roof surface (more in Lithuania is prudent because snow lies on flat/low roofs and meltwater ponds against upstands — judgement).
- Flashing options by main roof type: EPDM or bitumen membrane turned up the curb faces and over/under the top plate (best on flat roofs, all six outside corners need pre-formed or carefully folded/welded corners); on a pitched metal or tile roof, a back gutter on the upslope side(s) and apron on the downslope side in aluminium or lead (lead is heavy and toxic but very formable; aluminium needs mitred/riveted/sealed corners). The sheet overhang then laps over the top of the flashing like a counter-flashing.
- Insulate the curb sides (e.g. 30–70 mm PIR/XPS inside or outside the timber, as in the BuildHub detail) so its inner face stays above dew point; an uninsulated timber curb with a cold interior face is a classic condensation/mould spot under rooflights.

### Gaps
- No Lithuanian-specific guidance (STR) on rooflight upstand height was found; whether the curb sits on a flat or pitched main roof is unknown from the brief, so the flashing choice is generic.

## 5. Condensation: causes, ventilation, curb insulation, 20 mm multiwall U-value, dripping management

### Takeaway
Two distinct problems: surface condensation on the room-side face (indoor humidity meeting a cold sheet — managed by ventilation and warmer surfaces) and internal condensation in the flutes (moisture entering through badly sealed ends — managed by solid top tape, breather bottom tape and downslope flutes). 20 mm multiwall has roughly U ≈ 1.6 W/m²K, much better than single glass but worse than a modern insulating glass unit, so in Lithuanian winters some surface condensation is to be expected.

### Cited Findings
- Surface condensation (underside of sheet) is a ventilation/temperature issue; internal condensation in the flutes usually comes from moisture entering through poorly sealed ends — [PolycarbonateX: how to stop condensation in multiwall](https://polycarbonatex.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-stop-condensation-in-multiwall-polycarbonate-roofing-sheets-complete-guide) (snippet)
- End tapes are the most common cause of internal condensation; aluminium tape at top (blocks wind-driven rain), breather tape at bottom (lets moisture out); reversed or blocked tape traps condensation in the chambers — [PolycarbonateX](https://polycarbonatex.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-stop-condensation-in-multiwall-polycarbonate-roofing-sheets-complete-guide) (snippet)
- Roof pitch, breather tape, end closures and ventilation work together to let moisture drain out while keeping out dirt and insects — [Clear Amber AXIOME installers' guide to condensation](https://www.clearamber.com/brochures/1480678805AXIOMEINSTALLERSGUIDETOCONDENSATIONV1.01WEBV2.pdf) (snippet)
- Adding more tape or sealant to a surface-condensation problem traps more humid air; ventilate the space below instead — search summary of [PolycarbonateX: preventing condensation](https://polycarbonatex.co.uk/pages/how-to-prevent-condensation-in-polycarbonate-roofs) / [Zooplas](https://zooplas.co.uk/how-to-stop-condensation-under-polycarbonate-roofing-sheets/) (snippet; attribution uncertain)
- Polycarbonate is slightly hygroscopic and water-vapour permeable, so condensation can form inside the sheet even with good precautions; effects "purely visual and normally temporary" — manufacturer technical note, likely [Gallina: Condensation and multiwall polycarbonate](https://gallinausa.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/Condensation-web-brochure.pdf) (snippet; attribution uncertain)
- Russian guide: a dome shape lets condensate run down the walls rather than drip — [Polikarbo "Kupol" greenhouse](https://polikarbo.ru/teplitsy-iz-polikarbonata/teplitsa-kupol-v-forme-kupola/) (snippet)
- 20 mm 7-wall (7H) opal polycarbonate: U = 1.6 W/m²K, light transmittance 20%; 25 mm 7H: U = 1.4 W/m²K (clear 25 mm LT 55%) — [Ultraplast.lv 20 mm 7H opal](https://ultraplast.lv/shop/multiwall-polycarbonate-sheet/multiwall-pc-20mm-7h-opal-(2.8)-full-size); [Ultraplast.lv 25 mm 7H opal](https://www.ultraplast.lv/shop/multiwall-polycarbonate-sheet/multiwall-pc-25mm-7h-opal-(3.2)-medium-size) (snippets; distributor data, Latvian supplier relevant to Baltic market)
- Eurocode notes that roofs with U > 1 W/m²K can have reduced thermal coefficient Ct (snow melts) — see Q6 — [designforms.net snow coefficients](https://designforms.net/WebUser/Pub/Resources/Load/Snow/Coefficients%20for%20snow%20load.htm) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Rough dew-point logic (not sourced): with U ≈ 1.6 W/m²K, interior surface resistance ~0.10–0.13 m²K/W, room 21 °C and outside −15 °C, the inner sheet surface sits around 14–15 °C; indoor air at ~50% RH has a dew point ≈10 °C, so surface condensation is marginal in normal conditions but likely in humid rooms (bathroom, kitchen, drying laundry, new screed) or colder snaps (−25 °C). Surfaces most at risk are the timber hip rafters' room side, the curb inner faces and the aluminium eave/hip profiles (thermal bridges) — insulate the curb and keep metal on the outside only.
- Glass comparison (general knowledge, not sourced in this session): single glazing ≈5.7 W/m²K, standard double air-filled ≈2.8, modern low-E argon IGU ≈1.0–1.1 W/m²K. So 20 mm multiwall is roughly between double and modern low-E glazing.
- Dripping management on a 37.5° pyramid: condensate tends to run down the sheet underside to the eave rather than drip (steep pitch helps); give it somewhere to go — a small inner condensate channel/upstand lip on the inside of the curb, or a bevelled curb top that sheds it back outward under the eave profile. Keep room humidity controlled (extract fan / trickle ventilation); a small vented gap under the apex cap would admit snow and is not recommended in Lithuania (judgement).
- Opal (diffusing) sheet hides internal condensation and algae streaks better than clear.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (EN 16153-tested) U-value for a specific 20 mm 4-wall vs 7-wall product found; a GL multiwall datasheet listing EN 16153 U-values was garbled in the snippet — [AC Plastics GL multiwall product data](https://www.acplasticsinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Product-Data-GL-Multiwall-Polycarbonate.pdf).
- No building-physics paper specific to small polycarbonate roof lanterns in cold climates found.

## 6. Snow load in Lithuania and what it means for a 37.5° hexagonal pyramid; wind uplift

### Takeaway
Lithuanian sources give characteristic ground snow load s_k = 1.2 kN/m² (zone I) and 1.6 kN/m² (zone II) (STR 2.05.04:2003 / LST EN 1991-1-3 NA). For a 37.5° face, EN 1991-1-3 gives μ1 = 0.8·(60−37.5)/30 = 0.60, so roof snow s ≈ 0.72 kN/m² (zone I) or 0.96 kN/m² (zone II) on plan, before partial factors. Lithuanian wind values (vb,0) were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Vilnius city document: Vilnius is snow load region I under STR 2.05.04:2003, characteristic ground snow load 1.2 kN/m² (120 kg/m²), partial safety factor 1.3 for snow — [Vilnius.lt document](https://vilnius.lt/rest//assets/ff68c3b3-ef5f-4d4b-938b-e1f657897fac) (snippet)
- STR 2.05.04:2003 "Poveikiai ir apkrovos" contains section 1.7 on snow loads — [e-seimas: STR 2.05.04:2003](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/TAIS.213447/) (snippet; full text not accessible)

### Inferences
- Calculation for this pyramid (Ce = Ct = 1.0, EN 1991-1-3:2003 formula applied per face as a monopitch — the standard has no explicit hexagonal-pyramid case):
  - μ1 = 0.8 × (60 − 37.5)/30 = **0.60**.
  - Zone I: s = 0.60 × 1.2 = **0.72 kN/m²** (plan); design ×1.5 (EN γQ) = 1.08 kN/m², or ×1.3 (STR factor) = 0.94 kN/m².
  - Zone II: s = 0.60 × 1.6 = **0.96 kN/m²** (plan); design ×1.5 = 1.44 kN/m², ×1.3 = 1.25 kN/m².
  - Total characteristic snow on the whole 2.65 m² plan: ≈1.9 kN (zone I) / ≈2.5 kN (≈260 kg) (zone II); per triangular face ≈0.32 / 0.42 kN.
  - Per m² of sloping sheet the vertical load is s·cos37.5° ≈ 0.79·s (e.g. 0.76 kN/m² of sheet area in zone II), and the component normal to the sheet is ≈ s·cos²α ≈ 0.63·s (~0.60 kN/m² zone II) — these are what the 20 mm sheet must span between hip rafters.
- Unbalanced/drift case: wind can strip snow from windward faces and load leeward ones; EN uses 0.5·μ1 on one side for duopitch roofs — for a pyramid, assume one or two leeward faces at full 0.6·s_k with others at half. A 20 mm sheet spanning triangles of ≤1.01 m base is short-span and should be well within typical 20 mm multiwall load tables, but no table was verified.
- Snow sliding: at 37.5° on smooth PC, snow will slide off in slabs onto the curb/main roof — the eave drip should not be the thing that holds snow; check what lies below (path, door). Accumulated snow on the main roof around the curb can exceed the curb height on flat roofs — another reason for ≥150 mm upstand.
- Ct < 1 may be justified because U ≈ 1.6 > 1 W/m²K, but for a DIY design keep Ct = 1.0 (conservative; heating may be off).
- Wind uplift: on a small, steep pyramid the faces see suction on leeward sides and near hips/apex; fixing should be by continuous clamping at hips (screws at ~300 mm) plus a positive eave clamp, and the hip rafters must be mechanically tied (e.g. metal straps/angles) to the curb, and the curb to the roof structure, not just bedded in sealant.

### Gaps

- 20 mm multiwall load/span tables for triangular panels not found.
- No source for a 1.8 kN/m² Lithuanian value.

## 7. Safety and durability: not walkable; hail resistance of 20 mm multiwall

### Takeaway
Polycarbonate rooflights must be treated as fragile: HSE and specifier guidance say rooflights cannot be relied on to carry a person and falls through them are common. No standardised hail test result for 20 mm multiwall was found; only qualitative/warranty claims.

### Cited Findings
- Fragile rooflights are often hard to spot and should be treated as a serious hazard; protect anyone working on or near them — [HSE: falls through roof lights](https://www.hse.gov.uk/agriculture/experience/falls-rooflight.htm) (snippet)
- Rooflights are not built to carry a person's weight; they can become worn and brittle over time; treat any installed rooflight as fragile/unsafe — [Specifier Review: fragile roof lights](https://specifierreview.com/fragile-roof-lights/) (snippet)
- HSE inspector: rooflights are considered fragile because they cannot support someone walking on them — [Arco Services](https://www.arcoservices.co.uk/latest-news/common-misconception-triggers-rooflight-fall-on-farm-building/); October 2025: two companies fined over £95,000 after a worker stepped on a fragile rooflight — [HSE press release 2025-10-15](https://press.hse.gov.uk/2025/10/15/two-companies-fined-after-worker-falls-through-fragile-rooflight) (snippets)
- Controls: edge protection, safety nets, crawling boards/staging, mobile platforms, protective covers — [Roofing Today](https://roofingtoday.co.uk/fragile-roof-fall-lands-sole-trader-in-court); [Kee Systems](https://keesystems.com/?p=45784) (snippets)
- Hail: a distributor offers a "10 year limited warranty against yellowing and hail damage"; a Q&A says multiwall panels generally withstand normal hail when properly supported; Polymershapes and AmeriLux technical guides contain a "Hail Resistance" section but its content was not visible — [Lowe's multiwall PDF](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/292176d4-6874-47cf-92a7-8446dc20bab1/67284075.pdf); [Home Depot multiwall PDF](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/4e/4eefcf23-f385-4525-815b-0bbe6ddbde53.pdf) (snippets; which document holds the warranty claim is uncertain)

### Inferences
- Do all sealing/cap work from a ladder, scaffold or crawling board on the main roof, never kneeling on the sheets; the apex cap screw must be reachable without loading panels (e.g. fix cap before the last panel, or from inside through the opening).
- Snow clearing: do not climb onto or scrape the pyramid; at 37.5° snow will mostly slide; use a soft brush on a pole from the roof edge if needed.
- 20 mm multiwall with UV-coextruded outer face is generally considered more hail-tolerant than glass of similar weight (PC is ductile), but no tested figure found — treat as unverified.

### Gaps
- No EN 13583 / ball-drop hail test data for 20 mm multiwall found; no point-load (walkability) figure for 20 mm multiwall found.
